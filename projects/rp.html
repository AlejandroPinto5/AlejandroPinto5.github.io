Theoretical Investigation of the Ruddlesden-Popper Oxynitrides Sr₂(Ta,Nb)O₄₋ₓNₓ via Density Functional Theory

DFT study of structural, electronic, and phononic properties of RP-phase oxynitrides, searching for the most stable anionic orderings and identifying potential ferroelectric phase transitions.

// abstract

Abstract

Computational Physics DFT Study VASP SOD Code GUANE Supercomputer

The Ruddlesden-Popper (RP) phase is a perovskite arranged in dislocated layers, with an intergrowth of \(ABX_3\) perovskite layers and an \(AX\) layer: \((AX)(ABX_3)\). Partial replacement of oxygen by nitrogen leads to modifications of electrical, optical, catalytic and magnetic properties, with a possible substitution-induced ferroelectric behaviour anticipated.

This work presents the analysis of structural, electronic, and phononic properties of \(Sr_2(Ta,Nb)O_{4-x}N_x\) for two anion concentrations \(x=0.5\) and \(x=1.0\), achieved by means of DFT implemented in VASP, compiled in the "Guane" cluster of the supercomputer of the Industrial University of Santander.

The substitution was carried out through the SOD code obtaining 11 configurations for \(x=1.0\) and two configurations for \(x=0.5\). Structural relaxation found two minimum energy configurations with trans and cis anionic ordering, belonging to space groups \(Immm\) (S.G. 71) and \(Cmcm\) (S.G. 63), respectively. DFT calculations indicate the cis anion order is energetically more favourable. The phononic structure shows imaginary modes leading to a phase transition to the most favourable chiral soft mode with space group \(C222_1\) (SG. 20).

Search for the Most Stable RP Oxynitrides

This chapter presents a structural analysis of the oxynitrides RP phase \(Sr_2(Ta,Nb)O_{4-x}N_x\) and the search for the most stable anionic orderings. The anionic substitutions are carried out using the SOD package, a tool for computational modeling of periodic systems with site substitutional disorder.

A disordered system is modeled as a copy of the cell where different combinations of disorder are placed at the crystallographic sites. Each configuration different from the original is identified by its symmetry operations, and symmetrically equivalent configurations are grouped — only configurations with unique symmetry are considered.

Equivalent configurations and inequivalent configurations comparison.

The equivalence criterion is based on isometric transformations (translations, rotations, reflections). Two configurations sharing the same space group do not necessarily have the same energy — the energy depends on site occupancy and atomic interactions.

Anionic Substitution

The RP phase has the general formula \(A_{n+1}B_nX_{3n+1} (n=1)\), where \(A\) = Sr, \(B\) = Nb or Ta, and \(X\) = O. The two main crystal structures are \(Sr_2TaO_4\) and \(Sr_2NbO_4\), both represented by space group \(I4/mmm\) (139).

Cell parameters: \(a = 4.02994\) Å and \(c = 12.64448\) Å for \(Sr_2TaO_4\) (volume 205.35 ų); \(a = 4.04552\) Å and \(c = 12.74460\) Å for \(Sr_2NbO_4\) (volume 208.58 ų), consistent with experimental data.
